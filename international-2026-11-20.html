In 1997, northern France buried 31.2 tons of incinerator ash beneath a test road; 10 years later, copper, lead, and zinc had fallen to natural road levels

  • In 1997, researchers in northern France placed 31.2 tons of municipal solid waste incinerator bottom ash beneath a test road for a decade-long environmental study.
  • The road used a 25-centimetre subbase layer containing treated incinerator ash and other materials, while a comparison section used natural limestone.
  • Researchers collected leachate from both road sections and measured elements including copper, lead and zinc to assess metals escaping into the environment.
  • After ten years, copper, lead and zinc concentrations in ash-road leachate were approximately equal to those from the natural limestone comparison section.
  • The study was published in the Journal of Hazardous Materials, and ageing-related chemical reactions were linked to changes in how metals remained bound in the ash
